01 / Weighted ranking

Scores prioritize experiments. They are hypotheses to test against existing tools and actual usage. Recurring utility dominates at 50%. Demo potential is retained at 10% as a soft ordering influence and the first tie-break; it cannot lift a low-utility candidate over a high-utility one. Maintainability remains a major constraint.

All component scores use 1–5; higher is better. M = ease of maintenance. Weighted total = Σ(weight × score ÷ 5).

Tie-break: demo, then maintainability, then capability fit, then ID. Under the 1.1.0 weighting probe rises to second on recurring utility and maintainability, ahead of rows; probe is still a component-first experiment and its disposition, not its rank, governs whether it becomes a standalone tool. A failed acceptance gate cannot be offset by a high score.

02 / Candidate specifications

Each specification separates what the tool can observe, what it returns to the agent, and what it may change. Commands are proposed interfaces. Expand a candidate for its complete implementation boundary.

03 / Shared execution contract

The host enforces authority. Each tool narrows its own operation and produces evidence within that boundary. An unrestricted agent remains unrestricted even when these commands are available.

04 / Automated framework workflow

The first automated task is evidence gathering. Advance a candidate only after its load-bearing claim survives a locked protocol. Use the host's existing authorization; this document does not expand it.
